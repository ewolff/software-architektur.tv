# Leadership and Psychological Safety with Gitte Klitgaard

## Key Takeaways

- Psychological safety is about creating an environment where people are not afraid of negative consequences for speaking up, asking questions, or making mistakes.
- Leadership responsibility includes explicitly creating psychological safety for team members, not just setting direction but ensuring people can follow and feel supported.
- Psychological safety and comfort are not the same; safety should include productive discomfort, learning, and growth through challenges and intelligent mistakes.
- Building psychological safety requires courage from team members and leaders alike, combined with explicit communication about decision-making authority and expectations.
- Senior leaders and managers serve as role models and significantly influence psychological safety through their vulnerability, willingness to acknowledge mistakes, and curiosity about others.
- Psychological safety is a prerequisite for high-performance teams but alone is insufficient; it must be paired with clear expectations, accountability, and deliberate decision-making.

## Core Questions Addressed

- What exactly is psychological safety and how does it differ from comfort?
- What is the relationship between leadership and psychological safety?
- Why is psychological safety not more broadly implemented in organizations despite its documented importance?
- How can psychological safety be established and maintained in teams when management does not actively support it?
- How can leaders rebuild trust and psychological safety after breaking it through mistakes or inappropriate behavior?
- What role do biases play in either supporting or undermining psychological safety?

## Glossary of Key Terms

- **Psychological Safety**: A shared belief that team members can take interpersonal risks without fear of negative consequences such as punishment, humiliation, or rejection.
- **Fake Harmony**: A state where teams appear peaceful and agreeable on the surface but avoid productive conflict and the voicing of different perspectives or disagreements.
- **Management by Fear**: A leadership approach that uses threats, consequences, or fear to drive performance, which is effective short-term but unsustainable for building high-performing teams.
- **Intelligent Mistakes**: Errors that occur despite reasonable precautions and good decision-making processes, distinguishable from negligent mistakes resulting from failure to take obvious precautions.
- **Crew Resource Management**: A training framework, particularly used in aviation, that educates teams on how to communicate effectively and ensure all team members feel safe speaking up about concerns.
- **Delegation Poker**: A tool for transparently determining which team members take responsibility for specific project decisions and tasks based on their preferences and capabilities.

## Technologies mentioned

- **Project Aristotle** - Google's research initiative investigating the factors that make teams effective, which identified psychological safety as a key component.
- **Scrum** - An agile framework for team organization and project management that has become popular for its simple rules and recipe-like approach.
- **Extreme Programming (XP)** - An agile software development methodology that originally included courage as one of its core values.
- **Management 3.0** - A leadership approach framework by Jürgen Apello that includes tools like Delegation Poker for modern team management.
- **Amy Edmondson's Framework** - An academic research-based approach identifying three leader behaviors: frame work as learning, show fallibility, and be curious.
