## Key Takeaways

- Sam Newman sees AI as a magnifier: it surfaces poor communication, poor documentation and poor architecture, and reinforces practices we already knew were good
- Small changes are still good, says Sam: the teams that do really well with AI work in really small increments
- Allen Holub warns of a "huge leap back to waterfall": gigantic specifications before coding almost guarantee that the LLM builds the wrong thing
- Dylan Beattie sees a difference from the old waterfall: it took six months and $10 million to fail, now you can discover by tea time that you got it wrong
- Gitte Klitgaard wants teams to write down the right things, for example in a decision log, and to talk about value and the why instead of producing as much code as possible
- Jacqui Read doubts that writing things down helps when nobody understands the connections between the information
- Adele Carpenter asks for the same hygiene for documentation as for code: next to the code and in source control
- Avraham from the audience reminds the panel that much shared understanding happens in spoken language, which an LLM has no access to
- For Allen, AI is an amplifier that amplifies the bad really effectively; the companies that use it best already had solid engineering practices
- Agile was never about speed, says Allen; the only extra speed comes from not building what nobody wants
- Dylan: when friction is useful we call it traction; with zero friction, Formula One cars wouldn't move
- Adele values the friction of being challenged by colleagues: "You microdose the friction or you'll OD later."
- Allen sees real friction in organizations where management prevents the company from doing what it needs to do
- Sam: sometimes you have to go slower to go faster; the teams that got the most out of AI took the time to talk, experiment and share ideas
- A person from the audience adds friction on purpose: every tenth pull request gets an AI-generated quiz

## Glossary of Key Terms

- **Fishbowl**: A discussion format with open seats on stage; whoever wants to contribute takes a seat and joins in
- **Friction**: In Lucas Dohmen's introduction, for example having to ask the security team or a deployment that is hard to do; the panel also used the word for conflict, for being challenged and for whatever slows a team down
- **Traction**: What we call friction when it is useful, according to Dylan
- **Psychological safety**: An environment where people can disagree and argue, and it's fine, as Allen put it
- **Decision log**: A written record of decisions; Gitte's example is seeing what was decided after a week away
- **Human skills**: Gitte's preferred term for soft skills, "because it's not soft"

## Technologies and Works Mentioned

- **Claude**: Mentioned by Sam for teams that talk to each other instead of to Claude, and in a joke by Adele
- **ChatGPT**: Dylan's example of stale information: for a long time it named the wrong prime minister of the United Kingdom
- **RAG**: Allen would dump meeting transcripts into an LLM using RAG and search them later
- **Microsoft Word and Outlook**: Allen's and Adele's examples of user interfaces that keep changing
- **Artcraft**: Project described by Dylan that cloned office and graphics software with the same menus in the same place; name as heard
- **Extreme programming**: Gitte would go back to it and start from there
- **Scrum**: According to Allen, not even Scrum was about "twice the work in half the time"
- **Model-driven development and UML 2.0**: Jacqui sees spec-driven development repeating model-driven development; Sam adds "don't do UML 2.0"
