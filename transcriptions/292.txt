## About the episode

In this engaging live fishbowl session from the Software Architecture
Gathering, Vaughn Vernon, Cheryl Hung, Avraham Poupko, Eberhard Wolff,
and Ralf D. Müller tackle one of the most pressing questions in
the field: Is software architecture about human intelligence or
artificial intelligence? As AI tools increasingly design systems,
analyze code, and critique architectural decisions, the panel debates
whether these technologies augment or replace the architect’s
role. They explore the nuanced balance between machine-generated
patterns and human creativity, the ethical and accountability
challenges of AI-driven architecture, and practical ways architects
can thrive in an AI-augmented future. Audience participation ensures a
lively, thought-provoking dialogue on the evolving craft of software
architecture.

## Summary

At the Software Architecture Gathering, Eberhard Wolff hosted a fishbowl
with Cheryl Hung, Avraham Poupko, Vaughn Vernon and Ralf D. Müller.
People from the audience joined them on stage. The question: is software
architecture about human intelligence or artificial intelligence?

### How AI Changed the Work

Cheryl saw Arm move from caution to more openness. Arm's business is its
intellectual property, and it feared leaking it, but now there is more
support for AI in the ecosystem around its chips. Vaughn used to pay
developers tens of thousands of dollars a month, for example for web
UIs. Now AI does that work, without Vaughn having to know every detail
of React.js. Ralf compared working with AI to a glass labyrinth: "I
already see the exit, but I always hit some walls." Avraham wonders
whether there can be "language without thought", and whether he is being
lazy or using a tool.

### Will We Still Need Architects?

One person in the audience raised a hand when Eberhard asked who expects
no more software architects in ten years. Avraham would not predict it.
"I don't know" is a legitimate answer, and he rarely gets it from an
LLM. Eberhard objected that different training could fix that. For
Avraham it takes more: "I don't know" means not knowing whether one's
own experience is similar enough to the case at hand.

Vaughn reported that Claude keeps growing the same component until the
code is so complex that the AI itself starts to fail at changing it.
Constant refactoring is needed: "Craft [...] is not plentiful; it's
scarce." Cheryl doesn't expect fewer architects and asked what happens
if we end up with more, because any 16-year-old can generate beautiful
diagrams. Avraham replied that drawing is not what architects do; they
think. Cheryl added that they have to influence people and build a
common vision.

### Teams and Communication

Erik Bell from the audience asked how our understanding can keep up with
such fast tools. Ralf said that everybody becomes a builder, but not
everybody can verify the result. Vaughn doubted that LLMs will continue
to break through if people keep producing more of what they were trained
on.

Eberhard asked whether fewer people would make the communication problem
go away. Cheryl expects smaller teams, but maybe more companies. Avraham
holds that an LLM cannot be a first-class member of a team, because he
cannot build trust with it. Erik Bell said that problems are often
cultural and between humans. Ralf has Copilot take a first look at
issues. He does not see it as a team member, but does not know whether
there will still be human teams for coding.

An audience member pointed out that humans are distracted while LLMs
advance. For Vaughn, working with an LLM takes so much focus that the
day ends in exhaustion. Avraham suggested that this limit may be a
benefit, because it may force us to choose what matters.

### Mistakes and Their Costs

An audience member warned that people take the solutions ChatGPT knows
for all the solutions there are. Avraham told of a team that agreed to
refactor a big ball of mud before adding a feature. Encouraged by
Cursor's confidence, the development lead had it add the feature anyway.
The result was functionally correct, with more technical debt. "Cursor
is not a stakeholder." Vaughn suggested it could be seen as an
inexpensive experiment. Ralf found LLM mistakes cheap as long as reviews
catch them, and reminded the panel that humans make mistakes too. An
audience member added that trying out an idea has become much cheaper.

Richard from the audience disagreed. There are costs nobody pays in
tokens, such as ecological damage, intellectual property violations and
harm to societies. Avraham compared this to cheap transport, food and
clothing, whose hidden costs we now pay dearly.

### Responsibility

Mikhailo Brodsky from the audience asked who is responsible when an
architect can no longer understand the generated code. Richard said that
one shouldn't get into that situation in the first place. Cheryl thinks
that is where the world is going, with more disposable systems. Avraham
hopes for new disciplines, like those that construction developed for
skyscrapers. Vaughn expects that executives will find someone who is
responsible. Ralf pointed to vibe-coded open source projects that people
use, and named his own AsciiDoc linter, whose code he never checked.

### Conclusion

In the end Eberhard asked for a uniquely human skill to focus on.
Avraham named the ability to know what others are thinking, Richard
named empathy and agency, Cheryl storytelling, and Mikhailo Brodsky the
communication of decisions. Vaughn praised the critical thinking on the
panel. Ralf was careful: whatever we say LLMs cannot do, chances are
good that they will do it.

## Bullet points

### Key Takeaways

- Avraham Poupko values "I don't know" as an answer: it means not knowing whether one's own experience is similar enough to the case at hand, and he rarely gets it from an LLM
- Vaughn Vernon sees Claude grow monolithic components until the AI itself starts to fail at changing the code; regular refactoring is the answer, and craft is scarce
- Cheryl Hung asks whether there will be more architects rather than fewer, because anyone can generate beautiful diagrams; what counts is influencing people and building a common vision
- Ralf D. Müller describes AI as a glass labyrinth: the exit is visible, but you keep hitting walls
- Everybody becomes a builder, says Ralf, but you need to know how to verify what you build
- For Avraham, an LLM cannot be a first-class team member because he cannot build trust with it; Ralf finds Copilot helpful without calling it a team member
- Scarce human focus may force us to choose what is important, while an LLM diverges infinitely, says Avraham
- Avraham's example of a mistake: a development lead let Cursor add a feature to a big ball of mud although architects and developers had agreed to refactor first. "Cursor is not a stakeholder."
- Opinions differ on whether mistakes of LLMs are cheap: Ralf and an audience member point to reviews and quick experiments, Richard from the audience points to ecological and social costs that nobody pays in tokens
- Cheryl expects many more disposable systems
- Ralf warns against saying what LLMs are not capable of: chances are good that they will be

### Glossary of Key Terms

- **Fishbowl**: A discussion format with free seats on stage; whoever wants to contribute joins the panel
- **Vibe coding**: Letting an AI write code without really needing to understand the code
- **Big ball of mud**: A system without a clear structure; Avraham's example was a monolith that needed decoupling
- **Uncomfortably excited**: An expression Cheryl knows from Google for the feeling that a technological breakthrough is happening and nobody is sure how it will end up
- **Socio-technical systems**: The view that work can only be understood as team dynamics and technology together
- **Theory of mind**: The ability to know what other people are thinking, which Avraham names as a uniquely human skill

### Technologies and Works Mentioned

- **Claude**: Used by Vaughn to generate and refactor code; an audience member mentioned Claude Code
- **GitHub Copilot**: Ralf assigns it a first look at issues
- **GitHub Spark**: Ralf's example of solving a security problem with the platform's own login instead of one created by the AI
- **Cursor**: The AI tool in Avraham's example of an architectural mistake
- **The Mythical Man-Month**: Book by Fred Brooks, mentioned by Avraham when he explained why we have teams
- **docToolchain and arc42**: Ralf is the creator of docToolchain and involved in the arc42 documentation template
