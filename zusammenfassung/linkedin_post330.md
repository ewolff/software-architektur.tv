# 🪞 AI as a Mirror — What Software Development Can Learn About Itself

Just finished an enlightening conversation with Kevlin Henney about AI's role in software development, and it's NOT what most companies think it is.

**The core insight?** AI is a multiplier — but most organizations are multiplying the wrong things.

Here's what we explored:

🔍 **AI reveals our blind spots**: Like a mirror, it shows us what we're actually doing — and often, we're optimizing failure instead of value. Most developers spend 80%+ of their time on *failure demand* (fixing problems) rather than *value demand* (building new features).

⚡ **Speed isn't the answer**: While everyone pushes the accelerator with AI adoption, Kevlin argues we need to take our foot OFF THE BRAKE. Real acceleration comes from reducing technical debt and legacy code — not generating more of it faster.

🏗️ **Architecture matters more than ever**: Good separation of concerns, clear interfaces, and loose coupling? These principles benefit humans AND LLMs alike. The fundamentals that worked before? Still work now.

🧪 **Testing is the unlocked superpower**: AI can help us write better tests and documentation — but only if we use it for *quality*, not just speed.

💡 **For the next generation**: Learn to READ code, understand trade-offs, and know what's being generated on your behalf. Don't "vibe your way through."

The uncomfortable truth: Most AI adoption is about doing the wrong thing *better* rather than doing the *right* thing.

What's your experience? Are you multiplying value or failure?
