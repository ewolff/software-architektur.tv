[Avraham Poupko](https://www.linkedin.com/in/avrahampoupko/) from
[Imperva](https://www.linkedin.com/company/imperva/) (part of
[Thales Cybersecurity Products](https://www.linkedin.com/company/thalescloudsec/))
is a software architect from Israel who practices and
teaches software architecture. He has long been fascinated by how
people get together to create software, and for the last few years by
the new player on that stage: the LLM. In this episode he joins Ralf
D. Müller from Jerusalem, and the two of them are not alone. Claude
takes part as a third participant through the voice mode of the Claude
app. Avraham starts with a reverse Turing test and then gives Claude a
rule: don't speak unless spoken to first. From there the conversation
asks what humans bring to a team that an LLM does not. Avraham names
identity, mortality and the need for other people, and argues that
you can only be generous with a limited resource such as your time.
Claude pushes back that it might be "a teammate in the work, but not
in the meaning". Along the way we talk about purpose versus task, why
forgetting can be a feature, why onboarding an LLM shows how badly we
onboard people, and whether Christopher Nolan dreamed or hallucinated
his Odyssey. The episode was deliberately not rehearsed. It is also a
first look at the topic of Avraham's and Ralf's joint talk "Why
Agentic Coding Works" at the iSAQB Software Architecture Gathering in
Berlin this November.

**Use of AI in this episode:** Claude (Anthropic) took part in the
conversation through the voice mode of the Claude app. Its voice is
synthetic and its contributions are AI-generated.
